# Disciplinary Procedure

## 1. Principles

Discipline is handled consistently, proportionally to the severity of the issue, and always with the employee given a chance to respond before a final decision is made.

## 2. Stages

| Stage | When Used |
| --- | --- |
| Verbal warning | Minor, first-time issue |
| Written warning | Repeated minor issue, or a more serious first issue |
| Final written warning | Continued issue after a written warning, or a serious single incident |
| Termination | Gross misconduct, or failure to improve after a final written warning |

Some serious violations (e.g., the Code of Conduct's prohibited behaviours, safety violations putting others at risk) can go straight to a final written warning or termination without going through earlier stages, depending on severity.

## 3. The Process

1. The concern is documented and, where it stems from a report, may first go through a Workplace Investigation
2. The employee is informed of the specific concern and invited to a meeting, with the right to bring a colleague or People Ops representative
3. A decision is made and communicated in writing within 5 business days of the meeting
4. The employee can appeal within 5 business days of receiving the decision

## 4. Gross Misconduct Examples

Theft, fraud, serious harassment, violence or threats of violence, serious breach of confidentiality, and being under the influence of drugs or alcohol in a way that creates a safety risk. These can result in immediate termination, sometimes with suspension (paid) pending investigation.

## 5. Suspension Pending Investigation

Suspension during an investigation is precautionary, not a punishment, and is paid — it does not imply guilt.

## 6. Appeals

Appeals are reviewed by someone not involved in the original decision, typically the People Director or a more senior leader, and the result of the appeal is final.

## 7. Record Keeping

Warnings have an expiry: verbal warnings expire after 6 months, written warnings after 12 months, if no further issue occurs — they are not held against the employee indefinitely.

## 8. Relationship to a PIP

A performance-related concern (not a conduct issue) is generally handled through the Performance Improvement Plan Process rather than this disciplinary track, unless performance issues are compounded by a conduct element (e.g., falsifying performance data).
