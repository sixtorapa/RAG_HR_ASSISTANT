# Incident Reporting Procedure

## 1. What Counts as an Incident

Any workplace injury, near-miss, safety hazard, or home-office ergonomic concern. When in doubt, report it — under-reporting hides patterns that could prevent a future, more serious incident.

## 2. How to Report

Submit a report through the Incident Reporting Form on the HR portal within **24 hours** of the incident or discovery of the hazard. For anything requiring immediate medical attention, call local emergency services first, then report afterward.

## 3. What Happens After You Report

| Step | Timing | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acknowledgement | Within 4 business hours | People Ops |
| Initial assessment | Within 2 business days | Health & Safety Representative |
| Resolution or mitigation plan | Within 10 business days for non-urgent items | Facilities / IT / People Ops as relevant |

Urgent hazards (e.g., a blocked fire exit) are addressed immediately, not on this timeline.

## 4. Anonymous Reporting

You may report anonymously if you prefer, though this can limit the ability to follow up directly with you for clarifying details. Anonymous reports are still logged and reviewed by the Health & Safety Committee.

## 5. No Retaliation

Reporting an incident, including one caused by your own error, never results in disciplinary action for the act of reporting itself. Retaliation against someone for reporting is treated as a serious violation under the Disciplinary Procedure.

## 6. Quarterly Review

All incidents are reviewed in aggregate (anonymized where appropriate) at the quarterly Health & Safety Committee meeting to identify patterns and recurring risks across office locations.

## 7. Serious Incidents

Any incident resulting in significant injury or requiring external emergency services is escalated immediately to the People Director and Legal, in addition to the standard reporting flow, to ensure any legal or insurance obligations are met.
