# Promotion & Career Ladder Framework

## 1. The Levels

Our career ladder has five levels, consistent across all departments: Junior, Mid, Senior, Lead, Manager. Each level has published, role-family-specific competency expectations available on the internal Career Wiki.

## 2. When Promotions Happen

Promotions are decided twice a year, aligned with the Salary Review Calendar (February and August cycles). Off-cycle promotions are possible only for exceptional, time-sensitive cases (e.g., a role change required by a reorg) and require Department Head and People Director approval.

## 3. Promotion Criteria

A promotion requires:

- Sustained performance at "Exceeds Expectations" (3.5-4.5) or higher for at least two consecutive review cycles
- Demonstrated competency at the next level, not just excellence at the current level
- Manager nomination, submitted with concrete examples, at least 4 weeks before the promotion cycle

## 4. The Promotion Committee

Promotions to Senior are approved by the Department Head. Promotions to Lead and Manager go to a cross-functional Promotion Committee to reduce inconsistency between departments and managers.

## 5. What a Promotion Changes

A promotion typically brings a salary increase to at least the new level's band minimum, and may include a new stock option refresh grant (see Stock Options Guide) if not already vested past 50% of the prior grant.

## 6. If You're Not Promoted

Managers are expected to give specific feedback on what is missing for the next level, regardless of outcome. Being "not yet ready" is not a negative performance signal on its own — it is tracked separately from the Annual Performance Review score.

## 7. Lateral Moves

Moving to a different role at the same level (e.g., Senior Analyst to Senior Specialist) is an internal transfer, not a promotion, and follows the Internal Mobility & Transfer Policy instead of this framework.
