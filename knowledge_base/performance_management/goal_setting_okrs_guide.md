# Goal Setting & OKRs Guide

## 1. Why We Use OKRs

Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) connect individual work to company priorities. Every employee should be able to trace their quarterly goals back to a team or company objective.

## 2. The Quarterly Cycle

| Week | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Company OKRs published by leadership |
| Week 1-2 | Teams draft team-level OKRs |
| Week 2 | Individuals draft personal OKRs with their manager |
| Week 3 | OKRs finalized and logged in the OKR tool |
| Final week of quarter | Self-scoring + manager review |

## 3. Writing a Good Objective

An Objective is qualitative and inspirational ("Make onboarding the best part of joining the company"), not a number. Key Results are quantitative and verifiable ("Reduce time-to-productivity from 6 weeks to 4 weeks").

## 4. Scoring

OKRs are scored 0.0–1.0 at the end of each quarter:

- **0.7–1.0**: on track or achieved — this is the expected healthy range
- **0.4–0.6**: partial progress, worth a retrospective on what blocked it
- **0.0–0.3**: significantly missed — should be rare; if it happens often, the OKR was probably set wrong, not necessarily a performance issue

Scoring consistently at 1.0 every quarter is a signal that goals were set too conservatively, not a sign of excellent performance.

## 5. OKRs Are Not Performance Reviews

OKR scores are an input to your Annual Performance Review, but not the only one — they measure outcomes of specific goals, not overall role execution, collaboration, or growth. Scoring 0.5 on an ambitious OKR is not penalized the same way a missed core job responsibility would be.

## 6. Changing OKRs Mid-Quarter

OKRs can be adjusted mid-quarter if priorities genuinely change, but this should be the exception, logged with a short note explaining why, and agreed with your manager — not used to retroactively make a struggling OKR look achieved.

## 7. Who to Ask

Questions about how OKRs roll up to team/company objectives go to your manager. Questions about the OKR tool itself go to People Ops.
