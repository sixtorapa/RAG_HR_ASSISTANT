# Manager Calibration Guide

## 1. Why We Calibrate

Without calibration, the same performance can be scored differently by different managers. Calibration sessions align scores across teams before they are finalized and communicated.

## 2. When Calibration Happens

Calibration runs after individual managers submit draft scores, and before the Salary Review Calendar's "Compensation committee reviews budget" step — typically a 2-week window in December and June.

## 3. Who Attends

All managers within a department, facilitated by the Department Head, with a People Ops partner present to ensure consistency of process (not to override manager judgment on content).

## 4. The Forced Distribution Guideline

We do not use a strict forced ranking, but as a guideline, the expected distribution across a department is roughly:

| Score Range | Expected Share |
| --- | --- |
| Outstanding (4.5-5.0) | ~10% |
| Exceeds Expectations (3.5-4.5) | ~25% |
| Meets Expectations (2.0-3.5) | ~55% |
| Needs Improvement (1.0-2.0) | ~10% |

A department far outside these ranges (in either direction) is asked to provide written justification before scores are finalized.

## 5. How Disagreements Are Resolved

If managers disagree on a calibration adjustment for someone outside their own team, the Department Head makes the final call. The original manager is always informed of any change before it is communicated to the employee.

## 6. What Calibration Does Not Change

Calibration can adjust a score within reason but cannot be used to retroactively justify a PIP or a promotion decision that wasn't otherwise supported by documented evidence.

## 7. After Calibration

Final scores feed directly into the Salary Review Calendar and the Promotion & Career Ladder Framework cycles. Managers should not share draft (pre-calibration) scores with employees, since they may still change.
