# Employee Resource Groups (ERG) Guide

## 1. What ERGs Exist

Currently active: Women@Work, Pride Network, and Parents & Caregivers. New ERGs can be proposed by any employee with at least 8 interested colleagues, submitted to the DEI Council for approval.

## 2. Who Can Join

Any employee can join any ERG as a member or an ally — ERGs are not restricted to people who share the specific identity the group is built around, with the understanding that ally participants follow the group's norms around whose voices lead discussion.

## 3. Budget and Support

Each active ERG has an annual budget (set by the DEI Council, typically €2,000-€5,000 depending on size and planned activities) for events, speakers, or materials, separate from team L&D budgets.

## 4. Time Commitment

Participating in ERG activities during working hours is supported, within reason (similar treatment to other voluntary, work-relevant activities) — check with your manager for anything beyond occasional meetings to make sure workload is planned around it.

## 5. ERG Leads

Each ERG has 1-2 leads, who commit to a 1-year term and report a brief summary of activities and budget use to the DEI Council twice a year. Being an ERG lead is recognized as a leadership contribution and can be referenced in your Annual Performance Review development discussion.

## 6. Confidentiality

Participation in an ERG is never visible to your manager unless you choose to share it, and never factors into performance, compensation, or promotion decisions.

## 7. Starting a New ERG

Submit a proposal to the DEI Council including: purpose, estimated interested members, and a proposed lead. Approved ERGs get an initial starter budget and a dedicated Slack channel.

## 8. Company-Wide Events

Each ERG is invited to contribute at least one company-wide event or awareness activity per year (e.g., a panel, a learning session), funded separately from their standard annual budget.
